The latest installment from writer/director Hongjin Na, <cite>Hope</cite>, is a sci-fi action that feels like something we haven't seen since the early 2000's.

There are some common themes through all of Hongjin Na’s films. In <cite>The Chaser</cite>, we see not only the failure of the police to enact justice and save lives, but also how they actively work to stop the antihero from saving a victim. In <cite>The Wailing</cite>, we see organized religion (whether Folk or Christian) fail to secure salvation, and in fact it is only the personal moral choices of individuals that secure a fate. There is also a recurring exploration of xenophobia that is explored in The Yellow Sea and The Wailing especially.

Signs of Na’s authorship might then be sentiments of anti-institutionalism, pro-individualism, anti-collectivism, pro-anarchism. Think Nietzsche, or better yet, Tolkien. All of this is continued in <cite>Hope</cite> and it feels very coherent, subtle, and balanced.

I will personally never get tired of Hongjin Na writing this type of character and setting.

Jungmin Hwang plays a local cop in the small town of Hope Harbor circa 1995. In the wake of the North-South Korea divide, this tight-knit community must now deal with an additional foreign invader, similar to <cite>Jaws</cite> with the locals and tourists of Amity Island now facing the threat of the marine maneater.

In one moment in the first act, a ragtag group of local elders pick up Hwang in a truck. Hwang questions if their guns are registered (a recurring bit that always lands) and they rightfully give him a scolding.

Perhaps if the trailer put this setting and character more upfront rather than featuring monster action sequences, it would appeal to a broader American audience. After all, I felt like Hope Harbor was just like my hometown in Ohio. I knew that cop growing up, and I knew his cousin, and those townspeople. This was Hongjin Na doing something like JJ Abram’s <cite>Super 8</cite>.

Alongside our cop protagonist Hwang is a series of standout supporting performances. Hoyeon (known for her standout role in Squid Game) plays a rookie cop that of course ends up exhibiting more bravery and intelligence than most.

<cite>Hope</cite> is by far Na’s funniest film as well. Na often cuts his films with dry humor even in the darkest of stories. But he really went all-in this time on the eccentric small town characters.

The antagonist faction is all played by western performers; a subtlety that could be lost on the audience if not for the blue and green eyes of the characters. I won’t say more about this because there are several delightful surprises as the story unfolds.

The production design for <cite>Hope</cite> is immersive and transporting. Whether the ruined village, the moss-covered forest, or even just an office, the props, colors, and decor are gorgeous and authentic. There is also a whole setting and design of the antagonist faction that will surprise you.

The cinematography is also some of the best in Hongjin Na’s filmography. Na reunited with Kyung-pyo Hong from <cite>The Wailing</cite>. Hong was also the cinematographer on <cite>Parasite</cite>. What really set <cite>Hope</cite>’s cinematography apart were the expansive, larger than life shots composed in a handheld manner. Similar to <cite>The Brutalist</cite>, it just felt like a breath of fresh air at many points.

The action was extremely legible. The viewer is never confused about where things are and what’s happening. The only thing I can compare it to is James Cameron with <cite>Terminator 2</cite>. When the action is so The most impressive thing is how utterly thrilling Na can make some objectively small actions.

There were quite a few chase sequences that could possibly be trimmed down. That may push Hope a bit into <cite>Terminator 3</cite> territory. But honestly that is a matter of taste. I never looked at my watch once during my screening and I am always one to complain about movies being too long.

Some of the CGI also left me a bit puzzled. Hard to tell what was meant to be stylized and what may have been unfinished or rushed by a post-production contract that ran out of budget. If stylized, several images harken to <cite>Attack on Titan</cite>. It will be interesting to hear more about this aspect of the production as the years go on.

In some ways, Hongjin Na is like a Korean Tarantino, taking everything he loves and blending them up into the exact thing that he wants. In the case of <cite>Hope</cite>, we got a delicous <cite>Godzilla</cite>, <cite>Super 8</cite>, <cite>Aliens</cite>, <cite>Tremors</cite>, <cite>Avatar</cite>, <cite>Terminator</cite> smoothie. Not to say this is a derivative story with nothing unique about it. Quite the opposite. Go see it.
